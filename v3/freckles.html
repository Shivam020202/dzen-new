CLINICAL OVERVIEW

Freckles

Freckles are small, flat brown spots that usually appear on the cheeks, nose and other sun-exposed areas. They are harmless, but they often darken in the sun and can make the skin tone look uneven.

Freckles can look similar to other types of pigmentation, so a correct diagnosis matters. A personalised assessment confirms the type of pigmentation and the safest way to lighten it for your skin.

WHY IT HAPPENS

What Causes Freckles?

Freckles are mainly driven by genetics and sunlight. These are the most common factors.

Genetics

A tendency to freckle is inherited, and freckles often appear in childhood and run in families.

Sun Exposure

Ultraviolet light triggers extra pigment, so freckles appear or darken after time in the sun.

Skin Type

Some skin types produce pigment in small clusters, which shows as freckles.

Hormonal & Age Factors

Hormonal changes and years of sun exposure can lead to new spots or darker patches.

CARING FOR YOUR SKIN

Skin Care Do’s & Don’ts

Sun protection is the single most important step in managing freckles.

Do

  • Use broad-spectrum sunscreen every day
  • Reapply sunscreen when outdoors
  • Wear a hat and sunglasses in strong sun
  • Use gentle, brightening skincare as advised

Avoid

  • Tanning or long sun exposure
  • Skipping sunscreen on cloudy days
  • Unsupervised bleaching or lightening creams
  • Picking or scrubbing pigmented spots

When to See a Doctor

If a spot changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds, or looks different from your other freckles, see a doctor promptly so it can be checked.

CLEAR ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about freckles.

Are freckles harmful?
+
Freckles themselves are harmless. However, any spot that changes, bleeds or looks unusual should be checked by a doctor.
Can freckles be removed permanently?
+
Freckles can be lightened significantly, but sun exposure can bring them back. Daily sun protection is key to maintaining results.
Which treatment is best for freckles?
+
It depends on your skin type and how dark the freckles are. Peels suit lighter, widespread freckles, while laser may suit darker spots.
Is laser safe for Indian skin?
+
Laser can be used on many Indian skin types with careful settings, but there is a risk of temporary pigment changes, so treatment is planned conservatively.
How many sessions will I need?
+
Most people need a few sessions spaced several weeks apart. Your doctor will plan this after assessment.
How can I prevent new freckles?
+
Use broad-spectrum sunscreen every day, reapply it outdoors, and protect your face with a hat and sunglasses in strong sun.
